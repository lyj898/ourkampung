The short answer

For an aircon used daily, a general service every three months is the common guideline in Singapore's climate, and it's a standard clause in many tenancy agreements. A chemical wash or chemical overhaul is for when a general service no longer fixes the problem — persistent leaks, smells or weak cooling. A gas top-up is only needed if there's a leak, because refrigerant isn't used up.

How often to service

Singapore's heat and humidity make aircons work hard, and dust, mould and debris build up in the coil and drainage faster than they would in a drier climate. Every three months is the common guideline for units that run every day. If you only use the aircon occasionally you can stretch the interval — but not indefinitely, because mould grows in a unit whether it's switched on or not.

If you're renting, check your tenancy agreement: many make the tenant responsible for servicing at a set interval, and landlords often ask for the receipts at the end of the tenancy. More on what tenancy agreements usually ask for.

What the symptoms usually mean

Water dripping from the indoor unit

Most often a clogged drainage pipe, a dirty coil, or both — which a general service deals with. If it keeps dripping after a service, the unit may need a chemical wash, or the drainage pipe itself may need repairing.

Weak cooling

Start with the filters: clogged filters restrict the airflow and make the unit work harder for less. If clean filters don't help, a dirty coil or a refrigerant leak are the usual causes.

A musty smell

Usually mould or bacteria in the coil or the drainage. A general service may be enough; a smell that comes back quickly usually needs a chemical wash.

Unusual noise

Rattling, buzzing or squealing can mean a loose part, a failing fan motor or debris inside the unit. Get it checked rather than waiting for it to stop.

Why a gas top-up is rarely the answer

Refrigerant circulates in a sealed system. It isn't consumed as the aircon runs, so low refrigerant means there's a leak somewhere. Topping it up without finding and fixing the leak only buys time, and you'll be paying for another top-up soon. If you're told a unit needs gas, ask where it's leaking.

What you can do between services

  • Wash the filters every two to four weeks. Slide them out, rinse them under running water, and let them dry completely before putting them back.
  • Keep the outdoor compressor clear of plants, clutter and anything else that blocks airflow.
  • Run the fan or dry mode for a while before switching off, if your unit has one, to help dry out the inside of the unit.
  • Don't ignore a drip. Water damage to walls and ceilings costs more than the service.

After renovation, or before you move in

Renovation dust gets into aircon coils and filters, especially if the units ran during the works or weren't covered. Have them serviced before you start using them daily. The same applies if you're moving into a home and don't know when the units were last serviced. The post-renovation checklist covers the rest.

Questions to ask a servicing company

  • Is the price per unit, and is there a package for several units, or a service contract?
  • What exactly does the general service include — is the drainage pipe flushed?
  • If they recommend a chemical wash or overhaul, what did they find that a general service won't fix?
  • Is there any warranty on the work if the same problem comes back?
