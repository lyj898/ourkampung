How Much Does Face Painting Cost for a Kids' Party in Singapore? (2026 Guide)

Solo freelancer or agency, per-hour or per-package — here's what face painting actually costs for a kids' party in Singapore, and how to get real value for your money.

17 Aug 2026 · 6 min read

The short answer

Face painting for a kids' party in Singapore typically costs $150–$380 for a single booked session, depending on whether you go with a solo freelance artist or book through an agency or concierge, how long you need the artist on-site, and how many children are on your guest list. For context on how this fits your overall spend, see our guide to overall kids' party costs in Singapore — face painting is usually one line item within a broader entertainment budget.

Solo freelancer vs. agency or team booking

A solo freelance face painter found through classifieds or social media often charges $100–$180 per hour, which can look like the cheapest option on paper. The trade-off is reliability: if they fall sick, get stuck in traffic, or double-book, you have no backup and no one coordinating them with the rest of your day.

Booking through an agency or a concierge like OurKampung's face painting service usually runs a little higher — think $180–$380 for a coordinated session — but you're paying for a vetted artist, a backup plan if something goes wrong, and someone who briefs the painter on your timing and theme so they show up ready to go rather than needing directions on arrival.

Per-hour vs. per-package pricing

Most face painters price one of two ways. Per-hour is straightforward — you pay for however long they're on-site, usually with a one-hour minimum. Per-package bundles a fixed block (commonly 1–1.5 hours) with setup and breakdown included, which is easier to budget against and is how most agency bookings work. A few extras to watch for: a travel or transport fee of roughly $20–$40 depending on your location, and a premium of 10–20% for weekend or public holiday slots, since that's when demand is highest.

How guest count and party duration affect the price

One artist typically manages around 10–15 children per hour, depending on how detailed the designs are. If you've got a guest list of 30 or more young children, a single one-hour slot will leave a long queue and cranky kids waiting — you'll want either a longer booking or a second artist, both of which raise the cost. Most parties we help plan book 1 to 1.5 hours of face painting as one component of a two-hour celebration, which comfortably covers 15–20 children without anyone missing out on cake or games.

What's usually included and what costs extra

A standard booking typically includes the artist's time, professional cosmetic-grade paints, a set menu of designs to choose from, and basic setup and pack-down. What often costs extra: full-face designs or anything requiring more than a couple of minutes per child, add-ons like glitter or gems, sessions extended beyond the booked time, and travel to locations outside the artist's usual coverage area. It's worth confirming these details upfront rather than finding out mid-party.

Tips for getting good value

A few things make a real difference to what you get for your money:

  • Book early — good artists get snapped up for weekend slots weeks in advance.
  • Keep designs simple for parties with a lot of young or shy children, so the queue keeps moving.
  • Bundle face painting with other elements of your entertainment and décor through one concierge, so timings don't clash and you're not paying separate travel fees for every vendor.
  • Ask exactly what's included before you pay a deposit — the quote should cover time, paint quality and travel with no surprises on the day.
Good To Know

Common questions

Is face painting cheaper than hiring a magician?
Generally, yes — a face painting session typically costs less per hour than a magician, and it can run continuously in the background rather than needing a dedicated performance slot, which is why many parents choose it as their primary entertainment for younger children.
Do face painters charge more on weekends or public holidays?
Often, yes — expect a premium of roughly 10-20% for Saturday, Sunday and public holiday bookings, since that's when demand is highest. Weekday parties can sometimes secure a better rate.
