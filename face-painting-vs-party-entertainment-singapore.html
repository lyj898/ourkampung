Face Painting vs. Other Party Entertainment: Which Is Right for Your Kid's Party?

Every party has a finite entertainment budget. Here's an honest comparison of the most popular options in Singapore, so you can pick — or combine — the ones that suit your child's party best.

19 Aug 2026 · 7 min read

Why this decision matters

Most parties in Singapore budget for one, maybe two, entertainment elements — spreading further usually means each one feels rushed or underwhelming. Before you book anything, it helps to know what each option is actually like in practice, not just on a vendor's price list. Here's how the most requested options compare, so you can put your budget where your child (and their guests) will feel it most.

Face painting

Best for: ages 3 and up, mixed-age guest lists, parties where kids are also doing other activities.

Face painting is one of the most efficient entertainment dollars you can spend. It runs continuously rather than needing a dedicated performance slot, doubles as a take-home 'souvenir' that photographs beautifully, and gives shy or younger children a calm one-on-one moment rather than a big group show. The trade-off is queue management with a large guest list, and it lacks the single big 'wow' moment of a scheduled performance. See our face painting service for how we run it.

Magicians

Best for: ages 4–10, parties with a clear program and a moment for everyone to gather.

A good magician delivers a structured, memorable centrepiece — genuine gasps and a room full of attention on one act. The trade-off is cost (usually higher per booking than face painting or balloon twisting) and the need for a proper audience setup: seating, a clear sightline, and a slot in your run-of-show where everyone's actually ready to sit still.

Balloon twisters

Best for: ages 2–8, budget-conscious parties, younger guest lists.

Balloon twisting shares a lot of face painting's strengths — it's queue-based, produces a take-home item, and works well for younger children who might not sit still for a design. It's usually priced similarly to face painting and the two are sometimes offered by the same performer, alternating or splitting a station.

Mascot characters

Best for: theme-driven parties, younger children who love a specific character.

A costumed mascot delivers an outsized photo-op moment, especially for a strongly themed party like a superhero celebration. Appearances are usually shorter and priced higher per minute than face painting, so mascots work best as a brief highlight — a grand entrance, a group photo, a dance — rather than the entire entertainment slot.

Science and slime shows

Best for: ages 6 and up, parties that want an interactive, high-energy centrepiece.

These shows work like a scaled-down magician's slot — structured, presenter-led, and best suited to children old enough to follow along and get involved. They're a strong choice for tween birthdays where classic party games have lost their shine.

How they combine

These options aren't mutually exclusive, and some of the best-loved Singapore parties combine two: face painting running quietly in the background while a magician or mascot holds the main stage for 30–45 minutes, for instance. A common and well-priced combination is face painting plus one headline act from our wider entertainment roster — you get continuous activity for the whole party plus one unforgettable moment, without paying for two full headline bookings.

Budget tradeoffs at a glance

As a rough guide: face painting and balloon twisting each typically run $150–$380 per session; a magician, mascot appearance or science show typically runs $300–$600 depending on length and experience. Combining two elements naturally raises your total, so it's worth deciding your must-have moment first and building around it. If you're not sure what fits your budget and guest list, tell us your numbers and we'll suggest a sensible combination.

Good To Know

Common questions

Can we book both a magician and a face painter for the same party?
Yes, and it's a popular combination — face painting keeps children occupied throughout while the magician provides one focused performance slot. We coordinate the timing so neither competes with the other.
What age is too young for face painting?
Most artists are comfortable painting children from around age 3, once they can sit reasonably still for a couple of minutes. For younger toddlers, a simple design on the hand or arm usually works better than a full facial design.
