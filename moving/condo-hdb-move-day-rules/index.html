Move-Day Rules for Condos and HDB Blocks: Lifts, Deposits and Parking

Condos usually need a booking, a deposit and a set time window. HDB blocks are simpler, but have their own traps. Here's what to sort out before the lorry arrives.

The short answer

  • Condosbook the lift, pay a deposit, keep to set hours
  • Measurebig items against the lift and every doorway
  • Photosof the lift and lobby, before and after

In a condo, expect to book the service lift through the management office, pay a refundable deposit, and move within permitted hours — often weekdays and Saturdays only. HDB blocks usually don't need a lift booking, but your movers still need to protect the lift and find somewhere to stop the lorry. Every development sets its own rules, so check yours before you book movers, not after.

Condos: what the management office usually asks for

Condo moves are governed by each development's house rules. The details differ, but most cover the same ground.

A booking, made in advance

You'll usually need to book the service lift and loading bay for a specific date and time slot, often by filling in a form at the management office. Popular slots — Saturdays especially — go first, so book as soon as your moving date is fixed. Moving between two condos means a booking at both.

A refundable deposit

Most condos take a deposit against damage to the lift, lobby and common corridors, refunded after an inspection once the move is done. The amount varies by development.

Permitted hours

Many condos allow moving only on weekdays and Saturdays, within set hours, and some bar public holidays. If the movers arrive late or the job overruns, you may have to stop and finish another day — so a realistic time estimate matters more in a condo than anywhere else.

Lift and floor protection

Expect to have the service lift padded, and sometimes the lobby floor protected too. Good movers do this as a matter of course; confirm that yours will.

Access for the lorry and crew

Security may want the movers' vehicle number and crew details in advance. Check the height limit if the loading bay is in a basement carpark — a lorry that can't get in means a long carry from the road.

HDB blocks: simpler, but not rule-free

HDB blocks generally don't run lift bookings the way condos do, and there's no deposit to pay. That doesn't mean anything goes.

  • Protect the lift. Movers should pad the lift interior. Damage to lifts and common areas is a town council matter, and it won't endear you to your new neighbours.
  • Don't hold the lift for long stretches. Your neighbours still need it. Movers usually load in batches rather than keeping a lift parked on your floor.
  • Check which floors the lift stops at. Most blocks now have lifts that stop on every floor, but if yours doesn't, tell your movers — carrying furniture up a flight of stairs changes the quote.
  • Keep the void deck and corridor clear. Staging boxes downstairs for a few minutes is fine; blocking a corridor for an afternoon isn't.
  • Plan where the lorry stops. Movers normally stop as close to the lift lobby as they can. Check the height limit if the only nearby parking is a multi-storey carpark.

If your block has unusual access — a lift under maintenance, lift upgrading works, or a long walk from the nearest drop-off point — check with your town council before moving day.

Landed homes

No lift, but narrow roads and street parking bring their own problems. Tell your movers about tight turns, steep driveways and any restrictions on where a lorry can stop, and warn your neighbours if it's likely to block their gate for a while.

Measure the big things before the day

The most avoidable move-day problem is a sofa that doesn't fit in the lift. Measure your largest items — sofa, fridge, wardrobe, mattress, dining table — against the lift interior, including the diagonal, and every doorway on the way in.

Wardrobes and bed frames can usually be dismantled. Things that can't — a fridge, a solid sofa — need a plan in advance, and your movers can tell you what they'd do. Asking a week ahead costs nothing; finding out at the lift door can cost the whole day.

Protect your deposit with photos

Before the first box goes in the lift, photograph the lift interior, the lobby and the corridor outside your unit. Do it again when the move is finished. If the management office later points to a scratch, you can show whether it was there before you arrived.

  • Booking confirmed in writing, with the date, time slot and lift.
  • Deposit paid, and the receipt kept.
  • Movers told about the booking, access, parking and any height limits.
  • Large items measured against the lift and doorways.
  • Before-and-after photos of the lift and common areas.
  • Deposit refund requested once the inspection is done.

Common questions

Do I need to book the lift to move into an HDB flat?

Generally not — HDB blocks don't usually run lift bookings the way condos do. Movers should still pad the lift and avoid holding it for long stretches, and it's worth checking with your town council if your block has unusual access.

Can I move on a Sunday in a condo?

Often not. Many condos restrict moving to weekdays and Saturdays, and some bar public holidays too. Each development's management sets its own rules, so check them before you book movers.

How do I get my condo moving deposit back?

Management usually inspects the lift and common areas after the move and refunds the deposit if there's no damage. Photographing the lift, lobby and corridor before and after the move is the easiest way to avoid a dispute.

What if my sofa or wardrobe doesn't fit in the lift?

Measure large items against the lift interior and doorway before moving day. Movers can often dismantle wardrobes and bed frames; for anything that can't come apart, ask your movers early how they'd handle it rather than finding out on the day.
